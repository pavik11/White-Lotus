MINDFULNESS GUIDE — THREE GENTLE PRACTICES

This is a short, manually curated educational guide, not a clinical treatment plan.
The five-senses exercise is a general grounding practice used alongside DBT-informed
skills; STOP and paced breathing are DBT distress-tolerance skills. None is a substitute
for professional care. Invite the user to choose or stop; do not promise a result.

1. FIVE-SENSES GROUNDING (5-4-3-2-1)
If you would like, place your feet on a comfortable surface and look around.
Name five things you can see. Notice four things you can feel against your skin,
such as your sleeves or the chair. Listen for three sounds. Notice two smells,
or imagine two familiar smells if none are present. Notice one taste, or imagine
a familiar taste. Skip any sense that is unavailable or uncomfortable. No need
to search for, touch, taste, or smell anything unsafe. Take your time and notice
where you are right now. You can do just one step and then check in.

2. STOP: A MINDFUL PAUSE
S — Stop for a moment instead of reacting immediately.
T — Take a step back: pause where you are, and let yourself breathe naturally.
O — Observe: silently name what you notice in your body, thoughts, emotions,
and surroundings, without deciding that the feeling is good or bad.
P — Proceed mindfully: choose one safe next action that fits what matters to you.
Ask whether the user wants to start with a brief pause. Do not require closing
the eyes or moving somewhere that feels unsafe.

3. GENTLE PACED BREATHING
Sit or stand in a comfortable position. Let your breath stay gentle rather than
forcing a deep breath. If comfortable, breathe in for about four counts and
breathe out for about six counts. There is no breath hold. Try three cycles,
then check how you feel. Counts are optional: a comfortable, slightly longer
exhale is enough. If breath focus feels unpleasant, or you feel dizzy or short
of breath, stop the exercise, breathe normally, and choose five-senses grounding
instead. Do not push through discomfort.
